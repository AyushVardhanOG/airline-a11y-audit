# Summary of the accessibility audit

Generated by `src/analyze.py` from capture-scan run `20261111T142233Z` (saved copies of the pages, captured by hand on 11 Nov 2026). Numbers are from axe-core, WCAG 2.0/2.1 Level A and AA rules only.

## What was scanned

- **29 airlines**, **78 pages** (homepage, check-in, special assistance). Pages scanned per type: home 29, checkin 25, assistance 24.
- Excluded: Alaska (blocked_for_human_visitor). Pages that do not exist (MISSING) or are the homepage again (SAME_AS_HOME) were not scanned.
- Scan errors: 0.

## Main results

- **67 of 78 pages (86%) failed at least one rule.** Automated tools find only part of all accessibility problems, so a page with no failures is not proven accessible.
- In total **768 failing HTML elements** were found, on average **9.8 per page** (median 4; a few pages with many failures pull the average up).
- **27 different rules** failed at least once.
- By impact: critical 149 (19%), serious 614 (80%), moderate 5 (1%), minor 0 (0%) of the failing elements.

### Most common problems (by number of pages)

- **label-content-name-mismatch** (serious; WCAG 2.5.3): 46 pages at 23 airlines, 152 elements - Elements must have their visible text as part of their accessible name.
- **color-contrast** (serious; WCAG 1.4.3): 22 pages at 13 airlines, 228 elements - Elements must meet minimum color contrast ratio thresholds.
- **link-name** (serious; WCAG 2.4.4;4.1.2): 15 pages at 9 airlines, 45 elements - Links must have discernible text.
- **image-alt** (critical; WCAG 1.1.1): 13 pages at 8 airlines, 41 elements - Images must have alternative text.
- **nested-interactive** (serious; WCAG 4.1.2): 12 pages at 10 airlines, 34 elements - Interactive controls must not be nested.

### Airlines (average failing elements per page)

- Most: Peach 43.5, Cathay Pacific 41, SpiceJet 33.
- Fewest: JetBlue 0, Finnair 0.3, JAL 1.3.
- Full table: `summary_per_airline.csv`. Airlines have 1-3 scanned pages, so compare averages, not totals.

### Regions and page types (average failing elements per page)

- By region: India 20.9 (11 pages), Japan/Asia 19.4 (13 pages), Europe 6.6 (29 pages), North America 2.6 (16 pages), Middle East/Oceania 6 (9 pages).
- By page type: home 15.5 (29 pages), checkin 5.4 (25 pages), assistance 7.7 (24 pages).
- These groups are small (5-10 airlines per region), so differences between them are descriptive, not statistically tested.

## Automated access (blocking)

- **19 of 30 airlines (63%) did not show their real homepage to a headless browser**: 9 blocked it outright, 3 showed an error page, 7 cut the connection. Only 11 allowed it.
- This is why all pages were captured by hand with SingleFile instead of scanned automatically.

## Live page vs saved copy (method check)

For the 11 airlines that allowed automated access, 25 pages were scanned both live and as a saved copy, at most about 15 minutes apart.

- **16 of 25 pages** (64%) failed exactly the same set of rules in both versions.
- Average rule overlap (Jaccard, 1 = identical): **0.77** (median 1.00, lowest 0.00).
- Failing elements in total: **201 live** vs **197 in the saved copies**.
- Rules that failed only on the live page: color-contrast (3 pages), label-content-name-mismatch (1 page), aria-allowed-attr (1 page).
- Rules that failed only in the saved copy: label-content-name-mismatch (1 page), aria-allowed-attr (1 page), aria-prohibited-attr (1 page), button-name (1 page), image-alt (1 page), aria-command-name (1 page), label (1 page), link-name (1 page), aria-valid-attr-value (1 page).

## Limitations (state these in the paper)

- Automated checkers detect only part of all WCAG problems; manual testing is still needed.
- Saved copies are snapshots: menus, focus behaviour and anything after a click are not tested.
- Several homepages were redirected by location to their India version (the capture was made from India), so results describe what a visitor from India sees.
- One capture per page on one day; websites change often.
- 7 pages could not be found (MISSING) and Alaska could not be loaded at all.
