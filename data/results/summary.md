# Summary of the accessibility audit

Generated by `src/analyze.py` from capture-scan run `20261111T142233Z` (saved copies of the pages, captured by hand on 11 Nov 2026). Numbers are from axe-core, WCAG 2.0/2.1 Level A and AA rules only.

## What was scanned

- **29 airlines**, **78 pages** (homepage, check-in, special assistance). Pages scanned per type: home 29, checkin 25, assistance 24.
- Excluded: Alaska (blocked_for_human_visitor). Pages that do not exist (MISSING) or are the homepage again (SAME_AS_HOME) were not scanned.
- Scan errors: 0.

## Main results

- **67 of 78 pages (86%) failed at least one rule.** Automated tools find only part of all accessibility problems, so a page with no failures is not proven accessible.
- In total **768 failing HTML elements** were found, on average **9.8 per page** (median 4; a few pages with many failures pull the average up).
- **27 different rules** failed at least once.
- By impact: critical 149 (19%), serious 614 (80%), moderate 5 (1%), minor 0 (0%) of the failing elements.

### Most common problems (by number of pages)

- **label-content-name-mismatch** (serious; WCAG 2.5.3): 46 pages at 23 airlines, 152 elements - Elements must have their visible text as part of their accessible name.
- **color-contrast** (serious; WCAG 1.4.3): 22 pages at 13 airlines, 228 elements - Elements must meet minimum color contrast ratio thresholds.
- **link-name** (serious; WCAG 2.4.4;4.1.2): 15 pages at 9 airlines, 45 elements - Links must have discernible text.
- **image-alt** (critical; WCAG 1.1.1): 13 pages at 8 airlines, 41 elements - Images must have alternative text.
- **nested-interactive** (serious; WCAG 4.1.2): 12 pages at 10 airlines, 34 elements - Interactive controls must not be nested.

### Airlines (average failing elements per page)

- Most: Peach 43.5, Cathay Pacific 41, SpiceJet 33.
- Fewest: JetBlue 0, Finnair 0.3, JAL 1.3.
- Full table: `summary_per_airline.csv`. Airlines have 1-3 scanned pages, so compare averages, not totals.

### Regions and page types (average failing elements per page)

- By region: India 20.9 (11 pages), Japan/Asia 19.4 (13 pages), Europe 6.6 (29 pages), North America 2.6 (16 pages), Middle East/Oceania 6 (9 pages).
- By page type: home 15.5 (29 pages), checkin 5.4 (25 pages), assistance 7.7 (24 pages).
- These groups are small (5-10 airlines per region), so differences between them are descriptive, not statistically tested.

## Automated access (blocking)

- **19 of 30 airlines (63%) did not show their real homepage to a headless browser**: 9 blocked it outright, 3 showed an error page, 7 cut the connection. Only 11 allowed it.
- This is why all pages were captured by hand with SingleFile instead of scanned automatically.

## Rule check: experimental rules

- None of the rules in the results is tagged "experimental" by axe-core (checked against the installed axe-core's own rule list). All numbers are therefore from standard WCAG 2.0/2.1 A/AA rules.

## Live page vs saved copy (method check)

For the 11 airlines that allowed automated access, 25 pages were scanned both live and as a saved copy, at most about 15 minutes apart.

- **16 of 25 pages** (64%) failed exactly the same set of rules in both versions.
- Average rule overlap (Jaccard, 1 = identical): **0.77** (median 1.00, lowest 0.00).
- Failing elements in total: **201 live** vs **197 in the saved copies**.
- Rules that failed only on the live page: color-contrast (3 pages), label-content-name-mismatch (1 page), aria-allowed-attr (1 page).
- Rules that failed only in the saved copy: label-content-name-mismatch (1 page), aria-allowed-attr (1 page), aria-prohibited-attr (1 page), button-name (1 page), image-alt (1 page), aria-command-name (1 page), label (1 page), link-name (1 page), aria-valid-attr-value (1 page).

## Second checker: Pa11y (HTML_CodeSniffer, WCAG2AA) vs axe-core

The same 78 saved copies were also scanned with Pa11y, under the same offline conditions. The tools name their checks differently, so they are compared by WCAG success criterion (SC): for each page, which criteria each tool reports as failed. Pa11y "errors" only (its warnings and notices are "check by hand" items).

- Pages with at least one failure: **axe-core 67**, **Pa11y 78** (of 78).
- Findings: axe-core 768 failing elements; Pa11y 1576 errors.
- Of all 370 (page, criterion) failures found by either tool, **78 (21%) were found by both**, 89 (24%) only by axe-core and 203 (55%) only by Pa11y.
- Many criteria are only ever reported by ONE of the tools. Counting only the 5 criteria that both tools reported at least once (4.1.2, 1.3.1, 1.1.1, 1.4.3, 3.1.1): **78 of 177 (44%) found by both**, 16 only by axe-core, 83 only by Pa11y.
- Per page, the overlap of the two sets of failed criteria (Jaccard, 1 = identical) is 0.18 on average (median 0.17); identical on 0 pages.

Criteria flagged on the most pages (both / only axe-core / only Pa11y):

- 2.4.1 Bypass Blocks: 0 / 0 / 58
- 4.1.2 Name, Role, Value: 29 / 9 / 18
- 1.3.1 Info and Relationships: 14 / 5 / 28
- 2.5.3 Label in Name: 0 / 46 / 0
- 1.1.1 Non-text Content: 13 / 1 / 25
- 4.1.1 Parsing: 0 / 0 / 39
- 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum): 21 / 1 / 12
- 3.2.2 On Input: 0 / 0 / 21

Why they differ (checked on this data; details in docs/notes.md):

- **Same problem, different criterion**: a missing iframe title is 2.4.1 for Pa11y but 4.1.2 for axe-core; axe-core's link-name rule counts for both 2.4.4 and 4.1.2.
- **Criteria only one tool tests**: 4.1.1 Parsing (duplicate ids) only Pa11y - axe-core 4.14 switches its duplicate-id rules off by default and 4.1.1 was removed in WCAG 2.2; 3.2.2 (form without submit button) and obsolete `<font>` markup (1.3.1) only Pa11y; 2.5.3 Label in Name, 1.4.4 (zoom blocked) and 1.4.1 (links told apart by colour only) only axe-core.
- **Hidden elements**: Pa11y also reports hidden or zero-size elements (e.g. tracking-pixel images without alt text), which axe-core skips on purpose.

So low agreement does not mean one tool is wrong: they implement different tests. Using both gives wider coverage; neither replaces manual testing. Chart: `charts/checker_agreement_by_criterion.png`.

## LLM repairs (Objective 2)

Every de-duplicated failing element (one per airline, rule and identical HTML; not in iframes or shadow DOM; with a passing control run) was sent to each local model with the same prompt (temperature 0, seed 42). The fix was applied to a COPY of the saved page and re-scanned with axe-core. "Fixed" = the rule no longer fails on the element and no new violation appeared. Review flags mark fixed repairs that a person must judge (placeholder text, hidden content, removed ARIA, added/changed role).

- **qwen2.5-coder:7b**: 587 attempts, **361 fixed (61%)**, 212 not fixed, 13 fixed with a new issue, 1 invalid; answer unchanged 67 times. Of the fixed, **55 (15%) need a human check**.
- **llama3.1:8b**: 587 attempts, **350 fixed (60%)**, 213 not fixed, 20 fixed with a new issue, 4 invalid; answer unchanged 20 times. Of the fixed, **45 (13%) need a human check**.
- **gemma2:9b**: 307 attempts, **161 fixed (52%)**, 130 not fixed, 6 fixed with a new issue, 10 invalid; answer unchanged 51 times. Of the fixed, **23 (14%) need a human check**.

Rules with at least 10 attempts (all models together): hardest nested-interactive 4%, aria-required-parent 11%, aria-required-children 11%; easiest select-name 100%, link-name 100%, aria-command-name 100%.

Charts: `charts/repair_fix_rate_per_model.png`, `charts/repair_fix_rate_per_rule_model.png`, `charts/repair_human_check_share.png`. Passing axe-core is not proof that a fix helps real users; Objective 3 labels the fixes works / fails.

## Limitations (state these in the paper)

- Automated checkers detect only part of all WCAG problems; manual testing is still needed.
- Saved copies are snapshots: menus, focus behaviour and anything after a click are not tested.
- Several homepages were redirected by location to their India version (the capture was made from India), so results describe what a visitor from India sees.
- One capture per page on one day; websites change often.
- 7 pages could not be found (MISSING) and Alaska could not be loaded at all.
